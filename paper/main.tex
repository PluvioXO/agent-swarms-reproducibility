\begin{abstract}
Agent swarm evaluation must distinguish increasing the total token budget,
dividing a fixed ceiling, and changing coordination. We study these
comparisons in 8,400 controlled trials on fresh arithmetic and graph-colouring
tasks. At 4,096 completion tokens per worker, increasing independent width
from one to four improves accuracy by 16.0 percentage points on both families
while quadrupling the total ceiling. Splitting a fixed 4,096-token completion
ceiling instead lowers graph accuracy from 56.0\% to 18.5\%; the arithmetic
change is inconclusive. Primary comparisons do not establish an accuracy
advantage over a sequential control matched on scheduled calls and nominal
token ceiling. Ring communication provides no consistent gain. Eleven
complementary scaling studies add 20,376 held-out trials across seven task
families, including a fresh-task sixteen-worker confirmation that does not
establish superiority over fixed-four execution. Candidate and request
audits show that valid-answer recovery after initial failure depends on
token allowance; they also reveal incomplete worker activation and 97
controlled-study attempts with unknown usage. Resource controls, output
selection, and execution records thus qualify claims about agent scaling.
The findings support evaluating specific allocation policies; equal realized
compute advantages and general scaling laws remain unestablished from this work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Study and Findings}
We evaluate worker width as a resource-allocation decision
\citep{snell2024compute,kim2025agents}.
\textbf{Budget rules.}
Independent width $1\to4$ gains 16.0 pp on both families at fixed per-worker
allowance, quadrupling the total ceiling; fixing the total lowers graph
accuracy from 56.0\% to 18.5\% (arithmetic: $-24.0$ pp, inconclusive).
PlanBench's independent four-worker effect reverses from $-95.0$ pp
($[-98.0,-91.5]$) to $+10.5$ pp ($[6.0,15.5]$) as its ceiling doubles,
with higher-ceiling debit ratio 4.06. Across 148 complementary wider cells
(Figure~\ref{fig:evidence}), all use more recorded tokens: 75 score lower,
24 equal, 49 higher. These configurations share tasks and controls.

\textbf{Worker participation.}
BigCodeBench Muse's $N=16$ activation is 7.3\%, 45.3\% and 93.5\% at ceilings
32,768, 65,536 and 131,072. At 32,768, independent widths 1/8/16 pass
40/64, 36/64 and 14/64. Activation's causal role remains unresolved.

\textbf{Baseline choice.}
On fresh ZebraLogic tasks, transferred $4/8/16$ minus fixed four averages
$+0.92$ pp over the two higher ceilings ($[-0.58,2.50]$), establishing neither
superiority nor equivalence.

\textbf{Coordination.}
Sampling, aggregation and debate are distinct mechanisms
\citep{brown2024monkeys,wang2023selfconsistency,du2024debate}.
Countdown's independent gains of 9.5--17.0 pp use 2.77--3.85 times recorded tokens;
its pooled ring effect is $-5.0$ pp ($[-8.5,-1.75]$). Seven Muse graph zeros are
operational failures, not necessarily reasoning errors. These ring results
have narrower scope than shared-workspace communication
\citep{park2026discovery}; execution audits complement failure taxonomies
\citep{cemri2025failures}.

\textbf{Design and evidence.}
Figure~\ref{fig:worker-schedules} defines the controlled schedules and widths. Sources are BigCodeBench,
MBPP/EvalPlus, ZebraLogic, BBEH and PlanBench
\citep{zhuo2024bigcodebench,austin2021mbpp,liu2023evalplus,lin2025zebralogic,kazemi2025bbeh,valmeekam2023planbench};
custom graph/Countdown tasks use deterministic checks, with graph generation
inspired by Reasoning Gym \citep{stojanovski2025gym}. Complementary trials
number 26,648: 6,272 calibration and 20,376 evaluation. Task bootstrap retains configurations and repetitions, stratified where applicable \citep{efron1979bootstrap,field2007clustered}; 95\% CIs lack
cross-study multiplicity correction.

These results motivate evaluating allocation policies with recorded usage,
realized participation and competitive controls. Ongoing work tests policy transfer across resource
divisions; general scaling laws remain unestablished.\newline
