\begin{abstract}
Adding workers changes token use, worker participation and answer selection
as well as coordination. We examine eleven scaling studies with 20,376
evaluation trials across seven task families and 6,272 calibration trials,
using Luna and Muse Spark at requested widths up to sixteen. All 148 wider
configurations use more recorded tokens than their matched-ceiling
single-worker controls: 75 score lower, 24 tie and 49 improve. Budget-dependent
feasibility is visible on PlanBench: independent four-worker accuracy rises
from 0\% to 100\% when the ceiling doubles, while truncated stages fall from
99.94\% to 0.19\%. On BigCodeBench, Muse's sixteen-worker activation ranges
from 7.3\% to 93.5\% across three ceilings. A fresh 300-task ZebraLogic
confirmation transfers a communicating 4/8/16 policy without refitting, but
its advantage over fixed four remains uncertain (+0.92 percentage points;
95\% CI $[-0.58,2.50]$). Ring revision provides no consistent advantage over
independent revision. These heterogeneous studies use task-paired analyses,
study-specific output rules and amended operational protocols. Recorded
token debit does not measure exact total compute or retry expenditure.
The findings motivate evaluating allocation policies with execution records
and competitive controls; equal realized compute advantages and general
worker-scaling laws remain unestablished.
\end{abstract}

\section{Design and Findings}
We audit worker width as a resource-allocation decision
\citep{snell2024compute,kim2025agents}.

\textbf{Design.}
We analyse subsets of BigCodeBench, MBPP/EvalPlus, ZebraLogic, BBEH and PlanBench
\citep{zhuo2024bigcodebench,austin2021mbpp,liu2023evalplus,lin2025zebralogic,kazemi2025bbeh,valmeekam2023planbench},
plus custom graph/Countdown tasks with deterministic checks; graph generation
is inspired by Reasoning Gym \citep{stojanovski2025gym}.
Widths reach sixteen in coding and ZebraLogic; later diagnostics test one
and four. Model versions and routing vary. Figure~\ref{fig:worker-schedules}
distinguishes output rules; verifier-assisted gains include selection.

\textbf{Budget rules.}
PlanBench's independent $N=4$ effect changes from $-95.0$ pp
(CI $[-98.0,-91.5]$) to $+10.5$ pp (CI $[6.0,15.5]$) as its ceiling doubles.
Higher-ceiling debit is 4.06 times the single-worker control; stage truncation
falls from 99.94\% to 0.19\%. Figure~\ref{fig:evidence} summarizes 148 wider
cells sharing tasks and controls.

\textbf{Worker participation.}
BigCodeBench Muse's $N=16$ activation is 7.3\%, 45.3\% and 93.5\% at ceilings
32,768, 65,536 and 131,072. At 32,768, independent widths 1/8/16 pass
40/64, 36/64 and 14/64. Activation's causal role remains unresolved.

\textbf{Baseline choice.}
On 300 fresh ZebraLogic puzzles, transferred ring $4/8/16$ minus fixed four
averages $+0.92$ pp at 65,536/131,072 tokens ($[-0.58,2.50]$), establishing
neither superiority nor equivalence. At 131,072, sixteen communicating workers score
90.33\% versus four's 89.67\%, using 3.57 times retained debit.

\textbf{Coordination.}
Sampling, aggregation and debate are distinct mechanisms
\citep{brown2024monkeys,wang2023selfconsistency,du2024debate}.
Countdown's independent gains of 9.5--17.0 pp use 2.77--3.85 times recorded tokens;
its pooled ring effect is $-5.0$ pp ($[-8.5,-1.75]$). Seven Muse graph zeros are
operational failures, not necessarily reasoning errors. These ring results
have narrower scope than shared-workspace communication
\citep{park2026discovery}; execution audits complement failure taxonomies
\citep{cemri2025failures}.

\textbf{Uncertainty and limits.}
Task bootstrap retains configurations and repetitions, stratified where
applicable \citep{efron1979bootstrap,field2007clustered}; reported 95\% CIs
lack cross-study multiplicity correction. Pilots and smoke checks are excluded.
Retained debit omits retry expenditure and can include conservative charges
for unknown usage; equal realized compute is not established.

These cases motivate three checks for scaling claims: execution feasibility,
realized worker participation and competitive fixed-width baselines. Ongoing
work tests policy transfer across resource divisions.\newline
