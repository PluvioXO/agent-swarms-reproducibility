\begin{abstract}
Evaluating an agent swarm requires distinguishing the effects of increasing
the total token budget, dividing a fixed ceiling, and changing coordination.
We examine these comparisons in 8,400 controlled trials on fresh arithmetic
and graph-colouring tasks. At 4,096 completion tokens per worker, increasing
independent width from one to four improves accuracy by 16.0 percentage
points on both families while the total ceiling quadruples. Splitting a
fixed 4,096-token completion ceiling instead lowers graph accuracy from
56.0\% to 18.5\%; the arithmetic change is inconclusive. The primary comparisons
do not establish an accuracy advantage over a sequential control matched
on scheduled calls and nominal token ceiling. Ring communication provides no
consistent gain. Eleven complementary scaling studies add 20,376 held-out
trials across seven task families, including a fresh-task sixteen-worker
confirmation that does not establish superiority over fixed-four execution.
Candidate and request audits show that finding a valid answer after an initial
failure depends on the token allowance. They also reveal incomplete worker
activation and 97 controlled-study attempts with unknown usage.
The findings show how resource controls, output selection, and execution
records qualify claims about agent scaling. They support evaluating specific
allocation policies; equal realized compute advantages and general scaling
laws remain unestablished from this work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Study and Findings}
This ongoing research programme studies allocation, participation, and
selection. Controlled comparisons use widths $N=1,2,4$ (Figure~\ref{fig:worker-schedules}).
Complementary programming and ZebraLogic grids test $N=1,2,4,8,16$;
smaller diagnostic grids compare one and four. Tasks cover programming,
logical reasoning, arithmetic, graph colouring, and planning
\citep{zhuo2024bigcodebench,liu2023evalplus,lin2025zebralogic,
kazemi2025bbeh,valmeekam2023planbench}.
Paired bootstrap intervals retain whole-task clusters and study-specific strata.

Figure~\ref{fig:evidence} summarizes the complementary results. All 148
wider cells use more recorded tokens: accuracy is lower in 75, equal in
24, and higher in 49. At sixteen requested workers, BigCodeBench Muse's
activation rises from 7.3\% to 45.3\% to 93.5\% across ceilings of 32,768,
65,536, and 131,072. The transferred ZebraLogic $4/8/16$ policy gains
$+0.92$ pp over fixed four (95\% interval $[-0.58,2.50]$), establishing
neither superiority nor equivalence. The controlled fixed-total arithmetic
estimate is $-24.0$ pp; the comparison remains inconclusive.


The wider grids reveal failures beyond four workers. For independent
BigCodeBench Muse at $B=32{,}768$, accuracy is 36/64 at $N=8$, 14/64 at
$N=16$, and 40/64 at $N=1$. Programming contributes 96 wider cells, 81 flat or
worse, so the overall configuration count is not benchmark-balanced.

PlanBench's independent four-worker effect reverses from $-95.0$ pp
($[-98.0,-91.5]$) to $+10.5$ pp ($[6.0,15.5]$) as its ceiling doubles;
the higher-ceiling gain uses 4.06 times the control's retained debit.
BBEH ring effects similarly change from $-6.5$ to $+4.5$ pp.
Countdown's independent gains of 9.5--17.0 pp use 2.77--3.85 times the
recorded tokens; ring revision instead reduces its pooled accuracy by
$5.0$ pp ($[-8.5,-1.75]$). These results separate search gains from the
tested communication intervention.

Equal ceilings do not establish equal realized compute. Retained charges
are incomplete ledgers, including 97 controlled-study attempts with unknown
usage. Further work concerns resource-matched controls and policy transfer;
general scaling laws remain unestablished. Evidence and protocols:
\url{https://github.com/PluvioXO/agent-swarms-reproducibility}.
