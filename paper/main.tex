\begin{abstract}
Evaluating an agent swarm requires distinguishing the effects of increasing
the total token budget, dividing a fixed ceiling, and changing coordination.
We examine these comparisons in 8,400 controlled trials on fresh arithmetic
and graph-colouring tasks. At 4,096 completion tokens per worker, increasing
independent width from one to four improves accuracy by 16.0 percentage
points on both families while the total ceiling quadruples. Splitting a
fixed 4,096-token completion ceiling instead lowers graph accuracy from
56.0\% to 18.5\%; the arithmetic change is inconclusive. The primary comparisons
do not establish an accuracy advantage over a sequential control matched
on scheduled calls and nominal token ceiling. Ring communication provides no
consistent gain. Eleven complementary scaling studies add 20,376 held-out
trials across seven task families, including a fresh-task sixteen-worker
confirmation that does not establish superiority over fixed-four execution.
Candidate and request audits show that finding a valid answer after an initial
failure depends on the token allowance. They also reveal incomplete worker
activation and 97 controlled-study attempts with unknown usage.
The findings show how resource controls, output selection, and execution
records qualify claims about agent scaling. They support evaluating specific
allocation policies; equal realized compute advantages and general scaling
laws remain unestablished from this work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Study and Findings}
This ongoing study distinguishes sampling, aggregation, and debate
\citep{brown2024monkeys,wang2023selfconsistency,du2024debate}, with
allocation controls for task and architecture dependence
\citep{snell2024compute,kim2025agents}.
Controlled widths are $N=1,2,4$ (Figure~\ref{fig:worker-schedules});
programming/ZebraLogic grids use $N=1,2,4,8,16$, diagnostics $N=1,4$.
Task sources: BigCodeBench, MBPP/EvalPlus, ZebraLogic, BBEH, PlanBench \citep{zhuo2024bigcodebench,austin2021mbpp,liu2023evalplus,
lin2025zebralogic,kazemi2025bbeh,valmeekam2023planbench}.
Graph/Countdown tasks use deterministic checks; graphs adapt Reasoning Gym \citep{stojanovski2025gym}.
Paired bootstrap resamples task clusters, stratified where applicable \citep{efron1979bootstrap,field2007clustered}.

All 148 wider cells (Figure~\ref{fig:evidence}) use more recorded tokens:
75 score lower, 24 equal, 49 higher. BigCodeBench Muse activation at
$N=16$ is 7.3\%, 45.3\%, and 93.5\% at ceilings 32,768,
65,536, and 131,072. ZebraLogic $4/8/16$ minus fixed four is
$+0.92$ pp (95\% CI $[-0.58,2.50]$), establishing neither superiority
nor equivalence. Controlled fixed-total arithmetic: $-24.0$ pp, inconclusive.

At $B=32{,}768$, independent BigCodeBench Muse passes 40/64 ($N=1$),
36/64 ($N=8$), and 14/64 ($N=16$). Programming supplies 96 wider cells,
81 flat or worse; counts are not benchmark-balanced.

PlanBench's independent four-worker effect changes from $-95.0$ pp
($[-98.0,-91.5]$) to $+10.5$ pp ($[6.0,15.5]$) as its ceiling doubles
(higher-ceiling debit ratio: 4.06). BBEH ring effects change from
$-6.5$ to $+4.5$ pp. Countdown's independent gains of 9.5--17.0 pp use
2.77--3.85 times the recorded tokens; its pooled ring effect is $-5.0$ pp
($[-8.5,-1.75]$). Our ring protocol is narrower than shared-workspace
communication \citep{park2026discovery}.

Resource audits complement failure taxonomies \citep{cemri2025failures}.
Unknown usage in 97 controlled attempts limits accounting; equal ceilings
do not establish equal compute. Ongoing work tests matched resources and
policy transfer; general scaling laws remain unestablished. Evidence and protocols:
\url{https://github.com/PluvioXO/agent-swarms-reproducibility}.
