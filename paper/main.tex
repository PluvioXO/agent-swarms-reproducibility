\begin{abstract}
Agent swarm evaluation must distinguish increasing the total token budget,
dividing a fixed ceiling, and changing coordination. We study these
comparisons in 8,400 controlled trials on fresh arithmetic and graph-colouring
tasks. At 4,096 completion tokens per worker, increasing independent width
from one to four improves accuracy by 16.0 percentage points on both families
while quadrupling the total ceiling. Splitting a fixed 4,096-token completion
ceiling instead lowers graph accuracy from 56.0\% to 18.5\%; the arithmetic
change is inconclusive. Primary comparisons do not establish an accuracy
advantage over a sequential control matched on scheduled calls and nominal
token ceiling. Ring communication provides no consistent gain. Eleven
complementary scaling studies add 20,376 held-out trials across seven task
families, including a fresh-task sixteen-worker confirmation that does not
establish superiority over fixed-four execution. Candidate and request
audits show that valid-answer recovery after initial failure depends on
token allowance; they also reveal incomplete worker activation and 97
controlled-study attempts with unknown usage. Resource controls, output
selection, and execution records thus qualify claims about agent scaling.
The findings support evaluating specific allocation policies; equal realized
compute advantages and general scaling laws remain unestablished from this work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Study and Findings}
This ongoing study distinguishes sampling, aggregation, and debate
\citep{brown2024monkeys,wang2023selfconsistency,du2024debate}, with
allocation controls for task and architecture dependence
\citep{snell2024compute,kim2025agents}.
Controlled widths are $N=1,2,4$ (Figure~\ref{fig:worker-schedules});
programming/ZebraLogic grids use $N=1,2,4,8,16$, diagnostics $N=1,4$.
Independent revision uses own drafts; ring revision receives predecessor
information. The controlled sequential worker uses prior-candidate and
verifier feedback.
Task sources: BigCodeBench, MBPP/EvalPlus, ZebraLogic, BBEH, PlanBench \citep{zhuo2024bigcodebench,austin2021mbpp,liu2023evalplus,
lin2025zebralogic,kazemi2025bbeh,valmeekam2023planbench}.
Custom graph/Countdown tasks use deterministic checks; graph generation is inspired by Reasoning Gym \citep{stojanovski2025gym}.
Complementary studies retain 26,648 trials (6,272 calibration; 20,376 evaluation).
Paired bootstrap resamples whole tasks with configurations and repetitions
together, stratified where applicable \citep{efron1979bootstrap,field2007clustered};
95\% CIs lack cross-study multiplicity correction.

All 148 wider cells (Figure~\ref{fig:evidence}) use more recorded tokens:
75 score lower, 24 equal, 49 higher. BigCodeBench Muse activation at
$N=16$ is 7.3\%, 45.3\%, and 93.5\% at ceilings 32,768,
65,536, and 131,072. ZebraLogic $4/8/16$ minus fixed four is
$+0.92$ pp (95\% CI $[-0.58,2.50]$), establishing neither superiority
nor equivalence. Controlled fixed-total arithmetic: $-24.0$ pp, inconclusive.

At $B=32{,}768$, independent BigCodeBench Muse passes 40/64 ($N=1$),
36/64 ($N=8$), and 14/64 ($N=16$). Programming supplies 96 wider cells,
81 flat or worse; counts are not benchmark-balanced.

PlanBench's independent four-worker effect changes from $-95.0$ pp
($[-98.0,-91.5]$) to $+10.5$ pp ($[6.0,15.5]$) as its ceiling doubles
(higher-ceiling debit ratio: 4.06). BBEH ring effects change from
$-6.5$ to $+4.5$ pp. Countdown's independent gains of 9.5--17.0 pp use
2.77--3.85 times the recorded tokens; its pooled ring effect is $-5.0$ pp
($[-8.5,-1.75]$). Our ring protocol is narrower than shared-workspace
communication \citep{park2026discovery}.

Luna graph colouring's pooled ring effect is $-2.0$ pp ($[-4.0,-0.25]$).
Seven Muse graph zeros are operational failures, not necessarily reasoning errors.

Resource audits complement failure taxonomies \citep{cemri2025failures}.
Unknown usage in 97 controlled attempts limits accounting; equal ceilings
do not establish equal compute. Ongoing work tests matched resources and
policy transfer; general scaling laws remain unestablished. Evidence and protocols:
\url{https://github.com/PluvioXO/agent-swarms-reproducibility}.
